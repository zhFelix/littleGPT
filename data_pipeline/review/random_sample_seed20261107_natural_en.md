- domain: chemistry

~~~~text
Ammonia synthesis is a classic example of the tradeoff between chemical equilibrium and reaction kinetics. Lower temperatures favor a higher equilibrium yield of ammonia from a thermodynamic perspective, but if the temperature is too low the reaction becomes impractically slow. Higher pressure favors the side with fewer gas molecules, yet high-pressure equipment is more expensive and creates additional engineering constraints. A catalyst lowers the effective activation barrier and allows the system to approach equilibrium faster, but it does not create a new equilibrium constant. Real process design must therefore compromise rather than maximize a single theoretical quantity. Similar tradeoffs appear in batteries, combustion, pharmaceutical stability, and materials synthesis. Asking first where a reaction tends to go and then how quickly it can get there keeps thermodynamic and kinetic questions separate. That distinction is one of the most useful organizing ideas in chemistry.
~~~~
